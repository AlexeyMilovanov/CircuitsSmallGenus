# The minimal algebraic theorem needed for OQ3

## 1. The right target

Fix a width `W`.  Let `Mhat_W` be the finite transformation monoid on
`Q_W = {0,1}^W` generated by all possible actual order-respecting cylindrical
layer maps of this fixed width used in OQ3.  Thus the monoid depends on `W`,
not on the input length `n` or on a particular circuit.  Constants are allowed.
Multiplication below is function composition.

For the paper it is enough to prove the following theorem.

> **Theorem.** Every (local) group contained in `Mhat_W` is abelian.

Indeed, a finite monoid is called solvable when every group contained in it is
solvable.  Abelian groups are solvable, so the theorem makes `Mhat_W` a finite
solvable monoid.  Barrington--Therien then gives

`WP(Mhat_W) in ACC^0`.

Neither of the following stronger assertions is needed:

* every group in `Mhat_W` is cyclic;
* for every arbitrary `S subset Q_W`, all restrictions to `S` of monoid
  elements that permute `S` form a cyclic group (`H1` in the Lean
  development).

The proof below uses only the abelian half of HMV Proposition 13.  In
particular, HMV Lemma 11 and the cross-stratum synchronization needed to turn
an abelian group into one cyclic group disappear completely.

## 2. Constant-free certified circuits

A transition has cyclically ordered source and target vertices and a **common
arc word** `Omega`: source incidences are consecutive source blocks in source
order, and the same word is consecutive target blocks in target order.  Every
nonconstant target is an `AND`, `OR`, or `COPY` gate with one or two
predecessors.  Shared endpoints cause no ambiguity because occurrences in
`Omega`, rather than pairs of endpoint labels, are ordered.

Let `N_W` be the monoid of transformations computed by stacks of such
transitions without constants.  Internal layers may have at most `W` vertices;
the two boundary layers have the `W` coordinate vertices.  Equivalently one
may retain `W` vertices everywhere and fill unused positions by geometrically
harmless dummy copies.

Every map in `N_W` is monotone and fixes `0^W` and `1^W`.

## 3. The incidence form of HMV Lemma 9

For a bit vector `x` on any current cyclic layer, let `rho(x)` be the number of
maximal cyclic one-blocks, with `rho(0)=0` and `rho(1)=1`; on the two boundary
layers this is the corresponding function on `Q_W`.

> **Component lemma.** If `f in N_W`, then
>
> `rho(f(x)) <= rho(x)`.
>
> If equality holds, the one-components of `f(x)` are in bijection with the
> one-components `I` of `x`, and the component matched to `I` is exactly
> `f(1_I)`.  Consequently
>
> `f(x) = OR_{I in comp(x)} f(1_I)`.

It suffices first to consider one transition.  Make a bipartite incidence
graph whose left vertices are the one-components of `x` and whose right
vertices are the one-components of `f(x)`.  Join `I` to `K` if a true target
in `K` has an incoming arc from a source in `I`.

Every right vertex has a neighbour.  A true `OR` or `COPY` has a true
predecessor, and a true nonempty `AND` has only true predecessors and hence has
one to choose.

One left vertex cannot have two right neighbours.  Suppose incidences
`a -> c` and `b -> d`, with `a,b in I`, witness two of them.  The source blocks
belonging to the cyclic interval `I` form one interval `P(I)` of `Omega`.
Choose the segment between the two witness incidences that stays in `P(I)`.
Every complete target block strictly between the endpoint target blocks has
all predecessors in `I`, so its gate is true.  Together with the already true
endpoints `c,d`, this gives a path of consecutive true target vertices from
`c` to `d`; they lie in the same output component, a contradiction.

Thus the number of right vertices is at most the number of left vertices.  If
the numbers are equal, the incidence graph is a perfect matching.  For a
matched pair `I,K`, every predecessor of a true `AND` in `K` lies in `I`, and
every true `OR` or `COPY` in `K` has a predecessor in `I`; otherwise `K` would
have another neighbour.  Hence all of `K` is true on input `1_I`.  No target
outside `K` can then be true, since it would give `I` another neighbour.
This proves the equality statement.

For a stack, component count is nonincreasing at every layer.  Equality at the
end forces equality at every intermediate layer, so induction gives the full
statement.

## 4. The incidence form of HMV Lemma 10

> **Antichain rotation lemma.** Let `I_1,...,I_m` and `J_1,...,J_m` be
> antichains of nonempty proper cyclic intervals, each listed by cyclic start
> position.  If `f in N_W` and
>
> `f(1_{I_r}) = 1_{J_{pi(r)}}`,
>
> where `pi` is a permutation, then `pi` is a cyclic shift.

For one transition, the arcs whose sources lie in `I` form an interval `P(I)`
of the common word.  Each relevant `P(I_r)` is nonempty and proper: the empty
word gives the zero output, while the whole word makes every positive-fan-in
target true.  Moreover the `P(I_r)` form an antichain.  Inclusion
`P(I_r) subseteq P(I_s)` would, by monotonicity of every target gate in its
incident arc values, imply inclusion of the corresponding outputs.

We use the following elementary block-substitution fact.  Replace each point
`u` of a cyclic order by a consecutive block `P_u`, allowing empty blocks.
Sending the cut before `u` to the cut before the first nonempty block at or
after `u` is a degree-one weakly cyclically monotone map (on a cyclic lift it
increases by `|P_u|` when passing `u`).  It takes the leading cut of `I` to the
leading cut of `P(I)`.  On an antichain of proper substituted intervals these
images are distinct, because two proper intervals with one leading cut are
comparable.  Thus their cyclic order is the source-start order, even across
arbitrary runs of empty source fibres.

Now consider the leading cut of `P(I)`.  The first true target is a weakly
cyclic-order-preserving function `lambda` of that cut:

* if the cut lies in the incoming block of an `OR`, that target is the first
  true one;
* if it lies strictly inside the incoming block of an `AND`, that target is
  false and the next complete target block is the first possible true one;
* at a target-block boundary take the next target; `COPY` is the one-arc
  special case.

As the cut traverses one target block, `lambda` stays put or advances to the
next target, and over the whole word it has degree one.  At an internal `OR`
cut the current target is already true.  At an internal `AND` cut the current
target is false, and `lambda` points to the next block.  That block is either
fully included and true, or it is the trailing boundary block: a partially
included `OR` is true, while a partially included `AND` would leave the whole
output empty.  The assumed nonempty output excludes the latter case.  The
same check applies when the leading cut is between blocks.  Conversely the
proper-output assumption excludes the full-word case in which every target
is true.  Hence `lambda` is exactly the start of the output interval in all
boundary cases.

The leading cuts of `P(I_1),...,P(I_m)` therefore occur in source-start order,
and their `lambda`-images are the output starts.  Those starts are distinct,
because two proper target intervals in an antichain cannot have the same
start.  Weak cyclic order preservation is therefore strict on this family,
and `pi` is a rotation.  This includes `m=1,2` and wrap-around.

For a stack, every intermediate image is again a nonempty proper interval.  It
cannot become `0^W` or `1^W`, because the constant-free suffix fixes both
endpoints while the final image is proper.  Intermediate images remain
distinct, and they remain incomparable because a monotone suffix cannot turn
comparable inputs into an antichain of distinct outputs.  Apply the one-layer
argument successively; a product of cyclic shifts is a cyclic shift.

## 5. Groups in the constant-free monoid are abelian

Let `G` be a group contained in `N_W`, and let `e` be its local identity.  Put
`X = Im(e)`.  Each `f in G` restricts to an order automorphism of `X`; its
inverse in `G` supplies the monotone inverse.

Component number is invariant on `X`: apply the component inequality first to
`f` and then to `f^{-1}`.  Let `I(X)` be the finite set of nonempty proper
cyclic-interval configurations belonging to `X`.

There is one small point that should be stated explicitly.  If `x in X` and
`I` is a component of `x`, the equality case of the component lemma applied to
`e(x)=x` says that `e` permutes the finite component set of `x`.  This
permutation is idempotent because `e^2=e`, hence it is the identity.  Therefore
`e(1_I)=1_I`, and every component interval of every `x in X` itself belongs to
`X`.

The equality case applied to `f` now shows that the action of `f` on `I(X)`
determines `f` on all of `X`; the endpoints are fixed separately.  Since
`f=f o e`, its restriction to `X` determines `f` on the whole cube.

Stratify `I(X)` into successive inclusion-minimal antichains

`I_1, I_2, ...`.

Every stratum is invariant under `G`, because `f` and `f^{-1}` preserve order.
By the antichain rotation lemma, the action on `I_j` belongs to its cyclic
rotation group `C_|I_j|`.  Restriction therefore gives a homomorphism

`G -> product_j C_|I_j|`.

It is injective because the combined action on all interval elements
determines the transformation.  The product is abelian, so `G` is abelian.
No between-interval lemma and no synchronization of the shift amounts at
different strata is required.

## 6. Eliminating constants groupwise

Let `G` be a group in the full constant-bearing monoid `Mhat_W`, with local
identity `e`, and put `X=Im(e)`.  For every `f in G`, `Im(f)=X`: one inclusion
uses `e o f=f`, and the other uses `f o f^{-1}=e`.  Thus the set `K` of
coordinates constant on `X`, and their values `c_i`, is common to the entire
group.  Let `J` be its complement.

If `J` is empty, `X` is a singleton and `G` is trivial.  Otherwise define two
one-layer certified maps:

* `A` leaves coordinates in `J` unchanged and clamps coordinate `i in K` to
  `c_i`;
* `B` leaves every `j in J` unchanged and, in each cyclic gap following `j`,
  fills the coordinates of `K` by copies of `j`.

For `A`, the only arcs are the diagonal arcs at `J`.  For `B`, the target
fibres copied from each `j` are consecutive cyclic blocks.  Hence both maps
have common arc words.  They need only belong to the ambient certified class;
they need not be among the original generators of `Mhat_W`.

For `f in G`, set

`Phi(f) = B o f o A`.

On `X`, `A` is the identity, and `A o B` is also the identity.  Since every
`g o A` has image in `X`, this gives

`Phi(f) o Phi(g) = Phi(f o g)`.

The map `Phi` is injective.  If `Phi(f)=Phi(g)`, evaluate on `X`.  The map `B`
preserves all `J` coordinates and is injective on the subcube having the fixed
`K` coordinates `c`; hence `f=g` on `X`, and then `f=f o e`, `g=g o e` give
equality everywhere.

Finally, `Phi(f)` has no constant output coordinate.  For `j in J`, if
`(f o A)_j` were constant, then for arbitrary `x`

`f_j(x)=f_j(e(x))=(f o A)_j(e(x))`

would make `f_j` constant, contrary to the definition of `J`.  Every output of
`B` copies one of these nonconstant `J` coordinates.

Any certified stack computing a monotone transformation with no constant
output coordinate can be made constant-free by semantic constant propagation.
Process layers bottom-up, simplifying every successor before deleting a
constant vertex.  At each layer:

* remove a gate made constant by its inputs;
* delete an absorbing constant input together with the now-constant target;
* delete a neutral constant input, turning the target into `COPY` when one
  nonconstant input remains;
* restrict the common arc word by deleting the same incidences and restrict
  the cyclic vertex listings.

Every retained target has at least one retained predecessor.  Constant
vertices that have become irrelevant may simply be deleted, since width means
"at most `W`".  A wholly empty retained internal layer cannot separate the
initial layer from the nonconstant final outputs, because all retained gates
have positive fan-in.  The final layer retains all `W` coordinates because no
output coordinate is constant.  Thus `Phi(f)` belongs to `N_W`.

Consequently `Phi` embeds `G` into a constant-free group.  Section 5 makes that
group abelian, so `G` is abelian.

## 7. Final dependency chain

The complete paper-level algebraic chain is now

`certified cylindrical layers`

`=> every group of Mhat_W embeds in a constant-free group`

`=> every constant-free group embeds in a product of cyclic rotations`

`=> every group of Mhat_W is abelian`

`=> Mhat_W is a finite solvable monoid`

`=> WP(Mhat_W) in ACC^0`  (Barrington--Therien).

The only external theorem in this last implication is Barrington--Therien.
The component and antichain lemmas are the incidence-word version of HMV
Lemmas 9 and 10, with their proofs reproduced because the printed HMV
cylindricity predicate has the shared-endpoint mismatch.

## 8. Consequence for the current Lean strategy

The current `isCyclic_realizedSubgroup(S)` obligation is not the minimal
mathematical seam for the article.  A more faithful formal boundary is:

1. formalize the theorem that every actual local subgroup of `NonCrossing W`
   is abelian (or merely solvable), using its fixed idempotent identity;
2. invoke Barrington--Therien as the remaining literature black box to obtain
   `monoidWordACC_nonCrossing`.

If Barrington--Therien itself is to be proved inside Lean, a holonomy/cascade
development is still needed.  That is a separate formalization project, not a
missing step in the OQ3 paper proof.

## 9. Computational adversarial checks

The exact full-width `W=4` monoid currently enumerated on the VM has 3,551,466
elements.  As an independent check of the constant-free reduction:

* 1,466,202 elements fix both `0^4` and `1^4`;
* every one of them satisfies `rho(f(x)) <= rho(x)` for all 16 inputs;
* over all 69 antichains of proper cyclic intervals, all 6,445,140 instances
  in which such a map bijects one interval antichain onto another induce a
  cyclic shift;
* no counterexample was found.

These finite checks are not used as proof, but they test precisely the two
incidence lemmas used above, rather than the stronger and partly false
stabilizer invariants explored earlier.
